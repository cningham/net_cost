What immigrants contribute to public finances, by country of origin

Four countries publish how much tax immigrants pay compared with the public spending they receive. We combined the four studies into one score per country of origin. The score compares each group with natives.

How to read the score: it is the yearly difference from a native, per person, as a share of the country's income per head. 0% means the group contributes the same as natives. −10% means about US$ less per person per year (2023 prices).

Why countries differ

Groups from richer countries score higher. Groups from countries that produced many refugees score lower. Bubble size shows the peak share of that country's population who were refugees.

How it was done

  1. Collect each official study's figure per country of origin.
  2. Convert every figure into the gap with natives, as a share of that country's GDP per head. This removes currency, price and income-level differences.
  3. Combine them with a Bayesian model. Each study measures the same hidden score with its own scale, because some studies are lifetime and some single-year. Countries that only appear in Dutch regional groups are filled in from origin income and refugee history.
  4. Check the result. The ranking barely changes when any one study is left out (rank correlation ≥ 0.96). It also matches UK tax data that the model never saw (0.77).

Limits: the studies cover different years (2011–2022) and arrival cohorts, and only Finland adjusts for age. The Dutch study counts children; the others do not. Scores describe group averages in Nordic-style welfare states, not individuals.
