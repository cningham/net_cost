Net fiscal contribution of immigrants, by country of origin

Denmark, Sweden, the Netherlands and Finland put on one scale. Each value is the gap between an origin group and natives, per person per year, as a share of the host country's GDP per capita (Danish single-year basis). Bars show 90% credible intervals.

1. Ranking of origin countries

Zero = natives. 1% of GDP per capita ≈ US$ per person per year (2023 PPP, average of the four hosts).

2. Same origin, different host country

Published figures after conversion (gap vs natives, % of host GDP per capita), before the model. Countries reported by at least two of DK, SE, FI. Spread across hosts shows how much definitions and cohorts matter.

3. What explains the differences

Origin effect against origin-country income and against peak refugee outflow (refugees + asylum seekers per 1,000 population, UNHCR). Countries reported individually by at least one host.

4. How each study maps onto the common scale

Scale factor λ: how many units of the study's own gap correspond to one unit on the Danish annual scale. The Dutch study is lifetime NPV including children, so its factor is about 35 "year-equivalents".

5. Checks

Left: UK HMRC data (income tax + NICs paid minus tax credits + child benefit, per NIC payer, 2019-20) was never used in the model. Right: rank correlation of the origin effects with the main run under alternative specifications.

Method

  1. Common unit. For each host h and origin c: y = (net contribution of group − natives) ÷ host GDP per capita, same year. Removes currency, price level and host income.
  2. Model. One latent origin effect θc per country, measured by every study with its own shift and scale:
    yi ~ t5( αh + λh · mean(θ over countries in group i), σh )
    θc = β0 + β1·log GDPpcc + β2·log(1+refugeesc) + β3·EUc + τ·uc
  3. Anchor. Denmark: α = 0, λ = 1. Dutch regions measure the average θ of their member countries.
  4. Estimation. Bayesian (PyMC, NUTS, 4 × 1,500 draws), weakly informative priors. 120 observations; no divergences, R-hat ≤ 1.01.
  5. Not harmonisable. Lifetime vs annual (absorbed by one factor only), children counted only in NL, age-standardisation only in FI, different years (2011–2022) and arrival cohorts, birth country vs nationality definitions.
